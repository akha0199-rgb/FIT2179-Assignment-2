Malaysia's Regional Divide

Where you are born in Malaysia shapes how much you earn, how likely you are to be poor, and how easily you can find work. This page maps that divide across the country's 16 states and territories.

Income

Where the money is

Income is concentrated in the Klang Valley. Kuala Lumpur, Putrajaya and Selangor record the highest median household incomes, all above RM9,900 a month, shown by the darkest states. The further you move from this core, especially toward the east coast and East Malaysia, the lighter the map becomes, bottoming out in Kelantan at around RM3,600.

Poverty

Where poverty hits hardest

Poverty mirrors that pattern in reverse. The largest bubbles sit over Sabah (19.7%), Kelantan (13.2%) and Sarawak (10.8%), while the Klang Valley states almost vanish. Sabah's poverty rate is more than ten times Selangor's.

Jobs over time

Unemployment, year by year

Unemployment tells a similar regional story across time. Each row is a state and each column a year; redder cells mean more people out of work. The darker vertical band around 2020 marks the pandemic shock, when joblessness climbed in every state at once.

Recovery

Did things improve? 2017 versus 2025

Most states have recovered since the pandemic. Comparing 2017 with 2025, the lines for the majority slope gently downward or hold steady, but a few, led by Sabah, remain stubbornly higher than where they began.

Persistence

Who stays at the top of the queue

Ranking the states each year shows the divide is persistent, not a one-off. Sabah sits near the top of the unemployment ranking almost every year, while Selangor and Pahang stay near the bottom.

Scale

The shape of unemployment over time

Seen as a flowing whole, the total number of unemployed Malaysians swelled sharply around 2020 before narrowing again. The thickness of each band shows how much each state contributed, with the larger states naturally accounting for the most people.

Volatility

How much each state swings

Some states are far steadier than others. This shows the spread of each state's quarterly unemployment from 2017 to 2025: a wide box means the rate swung a lot, a narrow one means it stayed stable. Sabah and Labuan are both the highest and the most variable.

All together

Three measures at once

Pulling the three measures together, each line is one state across income, poverty and unemployment, scaled from lowest to highest. The healthiest states stay low on poverty and unemployment but high on income; the lines that climb from low income to high poverty trace the states being left behind.

The link

Does higher income mean less poverty?

The relationship is clear: states with higher median incomes have lower poverty. The points fall along a downward trend, and the East Malaysian states (orange) cluster in the low-income, high-poverty corner.

The bottom line

Above or below the national average

Measuring each state against the national average makes the outliers explicit. Bars to the right have above-average unemployment, Sabah, Labuan and Kelantan stand out, while the Klang Valley sits comfortably below the line. Wherever you look, the same handful of states appear on the wrong side of the divide.
